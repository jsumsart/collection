About

The collection and its public record

The JSU Department of Art Permanent Collection grew through decades of teaching, artmaking, exhibition, and preservation at Jackson State University. This page explains how the collection came to be, what it preserves, and how this public site supports research, teaching, and institutional memory.

How The Collection Began

The collection developed through the working life of the Department of Art. Objects entered university care through classrooms, studios, faculty offices, student exhibitions, visiting-artist programs, workshops, gifts, teaching purchases, and long-term acts of preservation. Rather than beginning as a single formal museum acquisition program, it grew out of the department’s teaching, exhibition activity, and care for the artistic history surrounding Jackson State University. Early faculty leadership, including Lawrence Jones and Maria Jones, helped shape the department’s visual culture, curriculum, and commitment to preserving works connected to campus life.

Mission

The mission of the collection is to preserve, document, and share the artistic life of the department and its wider community. It supports teaching and learning, makes works available for public discovery, and helps sustain the historical record of artists, faculty, students, alumni, and cultural networks connected to Jackson State University.

How To Read The Records

The records on this site draw from the active catalog, object study, departmental files, exhibition pamphlets, newspaper research, oral histories, artist interviews, and the shared memory of faculty, alumni, former students, and community members. Some entries remain partial or under revision, and that ongoing work is part of responsible public access.

Institutional Reach

The collection connects Jackson State to Johnson Hall, Farish Street, Lynch Street, Tougaloo College, Memphis, the Mississippi Delta, and wider networks of Black art education. It reflects how faculty and students built an expansive visual culture under conditions that often required care, resourcefulness, and long-term commitment.
