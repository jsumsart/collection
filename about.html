About

The collection and its public record

The JSU Department of Art Permanent Collection was formed through the working life of an HBCU art department. This site presents it as a teaching collection, an institutional archive, and a public record of Black artistic formation at Jackson State University.

What This Collection Is

The collection came into university care through classrooms, studios, faculty offices, student exhibitions, visiting-artist programs, workshops, gifts, teaching purchases, and acts of long-term preservation. It is not a conventional museum collection shaped by a single curatorial mandate. It is a pedagogical collection produced by the daily labor of the Department of Art.

Why It Matters

The catalog makes clear that HBCU art departments were not only training grounds for artists. They were also sites of collecting, exhibition making, canon formation, public pedagogy, and archival care. Finished works, studies, documentary photographs, and partially documented objects all belong to that history.

How To Read The Records

The records on this site draw from the active catalog, object study, departmental files, exhibition pamphlets, newspaper research, oral histories, artist interviews, and the institutional memory of faculty, alumni, former students, and community members. Some entries remain partial or under revision because the archive itself is uneven.

Institutional Reach

The collection connects Jackson State to Johnson Hall, Farish Street, Lynch Street, Tougaloo College, Memphis, the Mississippi Delta, and wider national and diasporic networks of Black art education. It documents how faculty and students built expansive visual curricula under conditions of segregation, uneven funding, and institutional constraint.
